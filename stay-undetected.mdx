Cheating always carries some risk, but the habits you build around how you play make a real difference. This guide covers the practices that meaningfully lower your chances of being flagged or banned while using EnmaCheats in Rust — follow them consistently and you will stand out far less to both anti-cheat systems and other players.

## General Habits

<Tip>
**Use a VPN.** Keep your main IP from being permanently banned. A residential-style VPN plan looks like a real home connection, so most servers won't flag it as suspicious traffic.
</Tip>

<Warning>
**Avoid rage features.** If staying unbanned is your priority, skip aggressive features like thick bullet, spread, and flyhack. Stick to ESP and the occasional aimbot — the less obvious your advantage looks, the safer you are.
</Warning>

## Feature Habits

Rust periodically runs sanity checks roughly every ten minutes. Triggering these checks with certain features is one of the fastest ways to get flagged or banned. Avoid the following features during normal play:

- Time changer / bright night
- Remove layers
- FOV changer
- Zoom
- Debug camera

<Warning>
**Don't use F7 to report other players.** Rust runs an environment sanity check when you open the F7 report menu. Using it while the cheat is active gives the anti-cheat a direct window to inspect your client.
</Warning>

## Look Legit

<Tip>
**Keep it believable.** Extreme stats and obvious aim snapping draw player reports, which in turn trigger manual reviews. Use smoother, more conservative settings — a kill rate that looks human will always attract less attention than one that looks automated.
</Tip>

<Note>
**Rage on a throwaway.** If you want to use aggressive settings, do it on an NFA account rather than your main. See the [NFA Loader setup guide](/game-accounts/nfa-loader) to get a fresh account ready.
</Note>
